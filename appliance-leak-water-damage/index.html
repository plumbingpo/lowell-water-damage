Appliance Leak Water Damage in Lowell: What to Know

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. A refrigerator ice maker line is a thin tube that can drip behind the unit for months. Cleanup is different for each of them.

For homeowners in Lowell, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Mop Up What You CanMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Leave the Door ShutA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A washer drain hose that slips out of the standpipe, or a clogged standpipe.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. Drywall there sags and stains while the insulation over it holds water.

Hidden Mold at the Back

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. Long before it is discovered, a slow drip back there can be growing mold across the floor and wall.

Floors and What Is Under Them

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Baseboards and Wall Bottoms

Drywall edges and baseboards pull up water that stands on a kitchen or laundry room floor. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

Move the Appliance and Inspect

We disconnect and move the appliance, confirm the supply is off, and trace the water path under cabinets, beneath flooring and into nearby walls with moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Remove Standing Water

We extract standing water out of carpet and off hard floors. To reach water caught below cabinets or under floating floors, our crew pulls toe kicks or takes up boards.

3

Save Materials by Drying

We set air movers and dehumidifiers and, where possible, dry cabinets and hardwood in place with directed airflow so they do not have to be torn out.

4

Sanitize and Take Final Readings

Surfaces touched by washer or dishwasher discharge get antimicrobial treatment. Final moisture readings are taken before we return the appliance, so it never sits on a damp floor.
